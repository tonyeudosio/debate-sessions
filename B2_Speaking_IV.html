Part 1Interview

The examiner asks each candidate questions from these topic areas (about 2 minutes). Sit facing your partner and take turns answering as if an examiner were asking.

Timing: approximately 2 minutes for both candidates.
Work & Study
  • What do you do — do you work or are you a student?
  • What do you enjoy most about it?
  • Is there anything you'd like to change about your daily routine?
Free Time
  • How do you usually spend your weekends?
  • Have your hobbies changed much since you were a child?
  • Do you prefer spending free time alone or with other people? Why?
Future Plans
  • Is there a skill you'd like to learn in the near future?
  • Where would you like to be, personally or professionally, in five years?
Part 2Individual Long Turn

Each candidate compares two photographs and answers a short question about them, without help from the examiner. The other candidate then answers a brief follow-up question.

Timing: 1 minute per candidate for the comparison, plus a 20-second follow-up for the partner.
Candidate A
These photographs show people living in very different places. Compare the photographs, and say what you think might be enjoyable about living in each place.

1-minute long turn
Follow-up for Candidate B (20 seconds): "Which of these places would you prefer to live in, and why?"
Candidate B

1-minute long turn
Follow-up for Candidate A (20 seconds): "Do you think having a garden or yard is important? Why, or why not?"
Part 3Collaborative Task

Talk together about the different factors that might make somewhere a good place to live, then decide which factor matters most.

Timing: 2 minutes to discuss, then 1 minute to reach a decision together.
What makes somewhere a good place to live?
Access to nature and green spaces
Being close to family and friends
Good public transport and services
A strong sense of community
Affordable housing
Job and study opportunities nearby
2:00
2-minute discussion
1:00
1-minute decision
Part 4Discussion

The examiner extends the topic from Part 3 into a broader discussion. Take turns answering, and react naturally to what your partner says.

Timing: approximately 4 minutes for both candidates.
  • 1.Do you think people's idea of a "good place to live" changes as they get older?
  • 2.Is it better to live in a place you know well, or somewhere new and unfamiliar?
  • 3.Some people say cities are becoming too crowded. Do you agree?
  • 4.How important is it to feel part of a community where you live?
  • 5.Do you think technology has made it easier to live far from your family?
4:00
4-minute discussion
