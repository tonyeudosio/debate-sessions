Why Do We Have to Wear Sunscreen?

Listening for detail + guided speaking · TED-Ed, ~5 minutes

Before You Watch

UV rays — invisible rays from the sun that can damage skin
SPF — a number showing how much a sunscreen protects you
sunburn — red, painful skin from too much sun
melanin — the substance that makes skin darker in the sun
broad-spectrum — protecting against two different kinds of UV rays
to reapply — to put something (like sunscreen) on again
Read these six words once before watching. You don't need to memorise them — just recognise them when you hear them. This is the only "preparation"; everything else comes from listening.

First Watch — Just the Gist

Quick check

In one sentence, in your own words: what is this video about?

No pressure here — this first answer is just to confirm you have the general idea. You already know you're good at this part.

Second Watch — Hunting for Details

Now watch again (or in short chunks, pausing where you need to). This time the goal is specific facts. It's completely fine to replay a section two or three times — that's not cheating, that's exactly how this skill is built.

Detail · Cause and effect

1. Which problem do UVB rays mainly cause?

Detail · Technical fact

2. What does the SPF number on a sunscreen bottle actually measure?

Detail · Open answer

3. In your own words: what does the video say actually happens inside your skin when you get a tan?

Don't worry about perfect grammar here — focus on getting the fact right. We'll go over it together live.

Detail · Numbers

4. Try to catch ONE number from the video (an SPF level, a percentage, a length of time — anything). Write the number and what it refers to.

Speaking — Using What You Heard

These tasks reuse the vocabulary and facts from the video, so you're not inventing language from nothing — you're recycling what you just heard. Speak for 1–2 minutes on each; I'll listen and we'll talk, not "correct" in real time.

Task A · Retelling (easiest)

Retell the main idea of the video in your own words, as if explaining it to a friend who never wears sunscreen. Use at least three words from the vocabulary list.

Tip: start with "Apparently, the sun sends out two kinds of rays..." — you don't need to remember every detail, just the shape of the explanation.

Task B · Personal connection

Think about a summer when you got sunburned, or a habit you have with sun protection. Describe it briefly — what happened, and what you do differently now (if anything).

This is the kind of question that often appears in real B2 speaking exams — talking about a personal experience.

Task C · Your opinion

Some people say sunscreen should be worn every single day, even in winter or on cloudy days. Do you agree? Why or why not?

There's no wrong answer here — this is about giving reasons and defending an opinion, a key B2 speaking skill.
