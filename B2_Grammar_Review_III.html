1. Articles

UseExample
a / anFirst mention; one of many → I saw a dog in the park.
theSomething specific / already known → The dog we saw yesterday was huge.
zero articleGeneral plurals/uncountables, meals, most countries → Dogs are loyal. / We had breakfast early.
the + superlatives/ordinalsthe first time / the best day of my life
the + nationalities as a groupthe French, the elderly, the unemployed
the + mountain ranges/rivers/seasbut zero article with single peaks → the Himalayas, the Nile but Everest

2. Quantifiers

QuantifierUse + Example
much / manyQuestions & negatives, more formal → How much time do we have? Not many people came.
a lot of / lots of / plenty ofInformal, affirmative sentences → We have a lot of time.
a few / fewCountable nouns → a few = some (positive); few = almost none (negative)
a little / littleUncountable nouns → a little = some; little = almost none
severalMore than a few, fewer than many → several attempts
both / either / neitherReferring to two items → Both books are good. / Either day suits me. / Neither answer is correct.

3. Confusable Verbs & Word Pairs

PairDifference + Example
say / tellsay + (that)/nothing; tell + object → She said she was tired. / She told me she was tired.
borrow / lendborrow = take (from); lend = give (to) → I borrowed her car. / She lent me her car.
make / domake = create/produce; do = perform a general activity → make a cake / do the housework
bring / takebring = towards the speaker; take = away from the speaker → Bring me the salt. / Take this letter to the post office.
remember / remindremember = recall yourself; remind = make someone else recall → Remind me to call her.
look / watch / seelook at = deliberate, still; watch = moving/changing over time; see = perceive, often involuntary
rise / raiserise = intransitive (no object); raise = transitive (needs object) → Prices rise. / They raised prices.
lie / laylie = intransitive, to rest (lie–lay–lain); lay = transitive, to put down (lay–laid–laid)

4. Question Tags & Indirect Questions

PatternExample
Positive statement → negative tagShe's a doctor, isn't she?
Negative statement → positive tagYou don't smoke, do you?
Imperative → will you?Close the door, will you?
Let's → shall we?Let's go for a walk, shall we?
I am → aren't I?I'm invited too, aren't I?
Indirect Wh- questionNo inversion, no do → Could you tell me where the station is?
Indirect Yes/No question+ if / whether → I wonder if you speak French.

5. Participle Clauses

FormUse + Example
-ing (active, replacing a relative clause)The man sitting by the window is my uncle. (= who is sitting)
-ing (reason, replacing because)Feeling exhausted, she went to bed early. (= because she felt exhausted)
-ed / past participle (passive)The books written by Orwell are famous. (= which were written)
Having + past participleAn earlier completed action → Having finished his exams, he went travelling.
Having been + past participleEarlier passive action → Having been warned twice, he was finally fired.

6. Inversion & Emphasis

Inversion after negative adverbials
Adverbial + auxiliary + subject + verb
Never have I seen such a mess. / No sooner had she arrived than it started to rain. / Not only did he apologise, but he also brought flowers.
👉 Used mainly in formal or literary style, for dramatic emphasis.
Cleft sentences
It + be + [emphasised element] + who/that...  |  What + clause + is/was...
It was John who broke the window. / What I need is a good rest.
👉 Used to highlight one specific part of the sentence.
NeutralEmphatic
I have never seen such a mess.Never have I seen such a mess.
We rarely see such dedication.Rarely do we see such dedication.
You should not open that door under any circumstances.Under no circumstances should you open that door.

7. Linkers of Purpose & Result

LinkerUse + Example
so that / in order that + can/couldWe left early so that we could catch the train.
to / in order to / so as toShe whispered so as not to wake the baby.
so + adj/adv + thatIt was so cold that we stayed indoors.
such + (a/an) + adj + noun + thatIt was such a cold day that we stayed indoors.
as a result / consequently / thereforeBegins a new sentence → It rained heavily. As a result, the match was cancelled.
