Part 2 — Long Turn
Long turn · No interruptions
✓ done

Comparing two ways of learning

Picture two classrooms. In one, students sit in rows, following a teacher at a whiteboard. In the other, each student wears a VR headset, working through a simulation alone. Compare the two situations, and say which you think leads to better learning — and why.
This is the real format: no photos here, just a vivid description — you'll get an actual image pair in the exam, but the skill is identical: compare, speculate, and reach a view, alone, for a full uninterrupted minute.
1:00
Time limit: 60 seconds
Comparison & speculation language
Whereas the first relies on..., the second seems to prioritize..."Whereas" is cleaner and more C1 than "but" for structured comparison — use it to open your comparing sentence.
What stands out to me is the level of independence each one demands.A strong opening for your opinion after comparing — signals you're synthesizing, not just describing.
It could be argued that the traditional setup offers more... whereas the VR one arguably offers more..."It could be argued" lets you present a balanced view before landing on your own — exactly what examiners want in a long turn.
Part 3 — Collaborative Task
Negotiate & decide
✓ done

Choosing one investment

Your school has budget for exactly one new classroom technology this year: interactive whiteboards, VR headsets, coding robots, or extra tablets. Talk through the options, weighing them against each other, and reach a decision.
Normally this is done with a partner. Practicing solo, argue both sides yourself out loud — for the exam, what matters most is the language you use to negotiate, not who "wins."
2:00
Time limit: 120 seconds
Negotiation language
I see where you're coming from, but have you considered...?Acknowledges a point before countering — the collaborative move examiners specifically listen for in Part 3.
Perhaps we should prioritize whichever has the broadest impact."Prioritize" plus a criterion is a clean way to steer a decision without dismissing other options.
What if we compromised and looked at cost per student instead?Reframing the criteria is a strong negotiating move — shows flexibility, a key Part 3 skill.
